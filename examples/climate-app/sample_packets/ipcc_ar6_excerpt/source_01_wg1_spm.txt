IPCC Sixth Assessment Report — Working Group I: The Physical Science Basis
Summary for Policymakers (Synthetic Excerpt for Demo Purposes)

SPM.1 — CURRENT STATE OF THE CLIMATE

A.1 It is unequivocal that human influence has warmed the atmosphere, ocean and land. Widespread
and rapid changes in the atmosphere, ocean, cryosphere and biosphere have occurred.

A.1.1 Observed increases in well-mixed greenhouse gas concentrations since around 1750 are
unequivocally caused by human activities. In 2019, atmospheric CO2 concentrations were higher
than at any time in at least 2 million years.

A.1.2 Global surface temperature has increased faster since 1970 than in any other 50-year
period over at least the last 2000 years. Global mean surface temperature increased by 1.1°C
(likely range: 0.8 to 1.3°C) over the period 2011–2020 relative to 1850–1900. The estimated
increase in global surface temperature since AR5 is principally due to further warming since
2003–2012.

A.1.3 Human-induced radiative forcing is 2.72 W/m² [2.17 to 3.27 W/m²] relative to 1750.
Emissions of CO2 are the dominant contribution. Over the period 1990–2019, effective
radiative forcing due to well-mixed greenhouse gases increased by 43 percent.

A.1.4 Atmospheric CO2 concentrations of 421 ppm, CH4 of 1908 ppb, and N2O of 334 ppb in 2022
are all unprecedented in at least the last 800,000 years as determined from ice cores.
The current atmospheric CO2 level is approximately 50 percent above the pre-industrial
reference level of 280 ppm.

A.1.5 The rate of global mean sea level rise has accelerated from 1.4 mm yr−1 over 1901–1971
to 3.7 mm yr−1 over 2006–2018, with continued acceleration observed since 2016.

CRYOSPHERE

The Arctic region has warmed at roughly twice the global average rate over the past 50 years.
Satellite observations confirm that Arctic sea ice extent has declined in every month of the
year since records began in 1979. The linear trend for September Arctic sea ice extent
(the annual minimum) is a decline of approximately 13 percent per decade.

The Greenland ice sheet has been losing mass at an accelerating rate since the 1990s.
Over the period 2010–2019, the average loss was approximately 280 Gt per year,
contributing about 0.77 mm per year to global mean sea level rise.

OCEAN HEAT

Ocean warming accounts for roughly 91 percent of the total energy accumulated in the
Earth system between 1971 and 2018. It is virtually certain that the global upper ocean
(0–700 m) warmed since the 1970s and it is extremely likely this extends to 2000 m depth.

SPM.3 — CLIMATE INFORMATION FOR RISK ASSESSMENT

The probability of at least one year exceeding 1.5°C above pre-industrial temperature during
the next five-year period (2023–2027) is estimated at 48 percent. This compares with near
zero probability estimated just a decade ago, reflecting the rate of ongoing global warming.
