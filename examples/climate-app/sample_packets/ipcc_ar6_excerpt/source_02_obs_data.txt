Observational Data Summary 2022

SEPTEMBER 2022 SEA ICE EXTENT

Arctic sea ice reached its annual minimum extent on September 18, 2022. The September 2022
minimum extent was 4.67 million km², placing it as the tenth-lowest extent in the satellite
record, which extends back to 1979. This was above the record minimum of 3.41 million km²
set in September 2012.

The five lowest September extents in the satellite record are: 2012 (3.41), 2020 (3.74),
2019 (4.14), 2016 (4.14), and 2007 (4.16) million km². The 2022 value continues the
long-term declining trend.

The linear rate of decline for September Arctic sea ice extent is approximately 13 percent
per decade relative to the 1981–2010 average baseline of 6.33 million km².

ATMOSPHERIC OBSERVATIONS — MAUNA LOA, HAWAII

Daily atmospheric CO2 measurements at Mauna Loa Observatory reached a daily maximum of
421.67 ppm on May 22, 2023. The monthly mean CO2 for May 2023 was 421.1 ppm, the highest
May value ever recorded at this station. The Keeling Curve record at Mauna Loa extends
back to 1958, and consistent records from ice cores push our understanding back 800,000 years.

The five-year mean growth rate of atmospheric CO2 for 2018–2022 was 2.5 ppm per year.

METHANE OBSERVATIONS

Atmospheric methane concentrations reached 1923 ppb in 2022 according to NOAA Global
Monitoring Laboratory measurements. This compares with a pre-industrial level of approximately
722 ppb. The 2022 value represents the highest methane abundance recorded in ice-core and
direct observational records extending back approximately 800,000 years.

OCEAN SURFACE TEMPERATURES

Global sea surface temperatures in 2022 were among the highest on record. The upper
2000 m of the world's oceans warmed at a rate of 0.13°C per decade over the period 2006
to 2020 according to Argo float measurements and other in situ data sources.
