Global Carbon Budget 2022: Policy Implications

This brief summarises key findings from the Global Carbon Project's annual carbon budget
assessment and the IPCC's remaining carbon budget estimates, with implications for near-term
policy action.

REMAINING CARBON BUDGET

The remaining carbon budget consistent with limiting warming to 1.5°C with a 50 percent
probability is approximately 500 GtCO2 from 2020 [1]. At current emission rates this budget
would be exhausted in roughly 12 years [2]. The carbon budget for a 67 percent probability
of staying below 1.5°C is even smaller, at approximately 400 GtCO2 [1].

CURRENT EMISSIONS TRAJECTORY

Global CO2 emissions from fossil fuels and industry in 2022 reached 36.6 GtCO2, close to the
pre-pandemic record high of 36.7 GtCO2 set in 2019 [2]. Land-use change emissions added a
further 3.9 GtCO2, bringing total anthropogenic CO2 emissions to approximately 40.5 GtCO2
for 2022 [2]. Emissions have grown at an average rate of 1.5 percent per year over the past
decade [1].

NET ZERO COMMITMENTS AND THE AMBITION GAP

Current nationally determined contributions (NDCs) submitted under the Paris Agreement
would result in emissions of approximately 53 GtCO2e per year by 2030, compared with the
43 GtCO2e per year required for a 2°C pathway [1]. To align with 1.5°C, global emissions
would need to fall by roughly 45 percent by 2030 relative to 2010 levels — a target that
current commitments fall significantly short of [1].

LAND AND OCEAN CARBON SINKS

Land and ocean carbon sinks together absorbed approximately 5.6 GtCO2 per year on average
over 2012–2021 [2]. The oceanic sink accounted for roughly 2.9 GtCO2 per year during this
period, while the terrestrial biosphere sink averaged 3.1 GtCO2 per year [2]. There is
growing concern that sink strength may weaken as warming proceeds.

NET-ZERO TIMING

The IPCC assesses that global CO2 emissions would need to reach net zero around 2050 to
give a 50 percent chance of limiting warming to 1.5°C [1]. Several large emitters have
announced net-zero targets, but the policies currently in place are insufficient to meet
these targets on the stated timelines [1].
